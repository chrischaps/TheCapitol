# The Capitol: The Commons Plan

*2026-10-21. Plan issue #352. Work packages #378 to #401, plus #311. Issues are labelled `commons`, with phase labels `cm:S0-groundwork` to `cm:S4-guilds`.*

## 1. Vision

The city is lived in now. Citizens keep human hours, stand on the hall's steps at market time, linger on the bridges at dusk and stop to turn to you as you pass. But nobody says anything. Every trade is a window of numbers. Every need is a bid on a book. What one person wants of another can't be said, promised, kept or broken.

**The Commons** gives the city its social half, in this order:
1. **Talk:** a line said where you stand, heard by those near you; one channel for the whole Capitol; and letters that wait for someone who's away.
2. **Commissions:** "20 iron ore, grade C or better, 6 Strands each, by the fourth day", pinned to the Gazette's board in the hall's forecourt with the money already down. A **claim** on one is a promise.
3. **Wages:** piece-rate work at someone else's forge, paid unit by unit as it's made.
4. **Standing:** whether you keep your word, said in words.
5. **Guilds:** a name over a hall, a shared purse, and dues to keep it.

The question the plan answers is this: **can the economy become a society?** Can a stranger's request, a promise kept and a name you come to trust make the Capitol feel like a place people belong to, and not only a market they visit?

It's also the economy's own fix. The town is short of demand. Strands pool with the gatherers and nobody buys (#294), and nobody walks to the wilds for ore (#336). A commission is demand you can see, and it pays the walk.

## 2. Goals and non-goals

**Goals**
- Local talk and a Capitol-wide channel, shown over heads and in a chat panel, and letters for people who are away.
- Delivery commissions with escrow and part delivery, posted and delivered at the hall's board, and claims that hold a share for a promise.
- Piece-rate wages at a station or on a lot. The pay is escrowed, so an employer can't breach.
- Standing in words from a record of promises, and weekly leaderboards in the paper and on the board.
- Guilds: a registry, roles, a treasury, dues, a hall in the guild district, and the Guild permission made real.
- Citizens in all of it: they speak, answer and post commissions, take wages and join guilds. They're marked honestly throughout.
- New sinks from the GDD's Contract Court and Guild Registry: the court's fee, postage, guild fees and dues.

**Non-goals for this plan**
- Custom contracts, sale contracts, disputes and arbitration (§8). Every contract here is one the server can check, so there's nothing to dispute.
- Citizens who hire, post wages or found guilds. *Since callings K2.4 (#538) citizens hire and post wages (D11); founding guilds stays out.*
- A word filter, shouting, or channels by zone.
- LLM layers in production beyond #311's spike.
- The React and Love2D clients. Both are frozen.

## 3. Where things stand (audit, 2026-10-21)

**There's no chat.** `meet.rs` and `Choice::Counter` both say so. `Bot::say` (`bot.rs:2012`) is a `println!`. `Bot::greet_people` (`:434`) stops and turns to a person, and says nothing.

**The parts exist:**
- **Interest.** Interest-based fan-out (#256, #275): `GameEvent` broadcasts filtered per socket by `INTEREST_CELL` (300 units, 3×3 cells). Local talk can copy `ExtractionStarted`'s filter (`connection.rs:951`).
- **Notices** that wait for an offline player (`engine/notices.rs`, `player_notices`, #138). Any `ServerMessage` can be queued and is taken once. This is mail's delivery.
- **The market's escrow** (`engine/market.rs`): one box, one transaction per action, and a conservation property test. It can't be reused as it stands, because `close` reads `market_orders`. Its parts can: `escrow_units`, `units::{take_units, insert_stack}`, `currency::*_strands_in`, `market_box` and the harness.
- **The ledger** (C1.7) counts escrowed Strands from `market_orders` only (`ledger::held_strands`, with the court's part in `court_held`). Each new table that holds Strands adds its sum there and points at it. New escrow must be added there, or it reads as drift.
- **The Gazette** (E3.9) composes a day from records, honours a byline opt-out (`players.gazette_named`), and its **notice board** already stands in the hall's forecourt. The board is a client prop: a click opens the paper (`hall.rs`, `cap_board_at`).
- **The hall:** `rules::market::at_the_hall` is where orders are placed (E3.4).
- **Guilds:** guild-district lots (#83, all `guild_hall`) and a `PermissionLevel::Guild` that `container_access` treats as owner-only "until those systems exist" (#80).
- **Citizens:** the `Decider` cascade, with `Question`/`Choice` menus, replay and Jev's prompts. A new task kind is a question, its choices, its rules scoring and its prompt text (the pattern in `sim/run/merchant.rs`).
- **One rate bucket.** Every WS command takes a token from a 60-a-minute bucket per connection (`rate_limit.rs`). Talk on that bucket would freeze a talker's walk.

**In the client:**
- **Names** over heads are HUD text placed by `labels::place` (clash test, fade, `MAX_NAMES` 24), drawn in `hud.rhai`.
- **World text** (#274) is depth-tested but has no wrapping and no clash handling, so it suits signs and doesn't suit bubbles.
- **A focused text field swallows keys** before the game sees them (Flint's text-focus gate), so typing never walks. Enter is `craft`.
- **The TDD's `Contract`** is a generic two-party model (Sale, Employment, Delivery, Custom) with disputes. This plan replaces it with commissions and wages (D5 to D7).

## 4. Design decisions

Chris made the four choices on 2026-10-21: chat's reach, which contracts come first, guilds as the last phase, and standing in words. The rest follow from them.

### D1. Talk's reach (Chris's choice)

`say { channel: local | capitol | guild, text }` and `said { from, name, citizen, channel, text, x, y }`.
- **Local** is heard within 30 units (`rules::talk::HEARD`). It's filtered by interest cell, then by distance.
- **Capitol** goes to everyone.
- **Guild** (S4.4) goes to the speaker's guild's members wherever they are, watchers too, and to no one else. Someone in no guild is refused. Guild lines aren't kept, so a socket that joins later doesn't get a backlog of them.
- **Nothing is stored.** The last 40 Capitol lines live in memory and are sent to a new subscriber.
- **Watchers.** A session that doesn't hold the avatar (E0.8) can read and post Capitol lines, but not local ones: you have to be somewhere to be overheard.

### D2. Text rules

- `rules::talk::clean`: 240 characters, control characters and newlines stripped, runs of whitespace folded, empty lines refused. Letters keep their newlines, up to 1000 characters.
- **Its own bucket:** `Category::Chat`, 8 lines per 10 s per account, so talking never costs a step.
- **No word filter.**
- **Mutes are per account, on the server** (`player_mutes`). A muted line or letter never arrives on any device, and a muted sender can't tell.

### D3. Speech in the client

- **Bubbles.** A line shows over its speaker for 4 s plus 60 ms a character (12 s at most). `PlayerLabel` and `labels::place` carry it, so the clash test counts a bubble's box. A speaker within earshot always gets a bubble.
- **The chat panel** has Local and Capitol tabs, names that open the person's card, and the last three lines when collapsed.
- **Enter opens chat,** except while the crafting panel or an open station is set to a recipe, where Enter still crafts. (As built, S1.3: the crafting panel is open from the start, so "while it's open" would leave Enter nothing to open chat with.)
- **Direct mode.** Opening chat frees the cursor, as any panel does (Controls D2).

### D4. Letters

- `POST /letters` to a player, by id (from a card's Write or a letter's Reply), since names aren't unique ([#428](https://github.com/chrischaps/TheCapitol/issues/428)). **Postage is 1 Strand**, a new sink that also keeps letters from being spam.
- Delivered with `notices::queue`, so a letter waits for an offline reader.
- Each box keeps 200 letters; the oldest read letters go first.
- Citizens answer letters from their phrasebook (S1.6), and later in their own words (#311).

### D5. Commissions (Chris's choice: delivery first)

A poster asks for N units of an item type, at or over a grade, at a price per unit, due in 1 to 7 game days.
- **The money is down.** The whole price is escrowed when it's posted.
- **The court's fee** is max(5, 1%) + 0.5% of the value (GDD §8.4's filing and escrow fees), all a sink. `rules::court::court_fee` is the one formula; clients preview with it.
- **At the board.** Posting and delivering happen at the hall (`at_the_hall`). Reading, claiming and cancelling work from anywhere.
- **Delivery.** Part deliveries are welcome. Delivered units go to the poster's market box, each keeping its own quality and maker's mark, and each is paid from escrow in the same transaction.
- **Closing.** Cancelling or expiring refunds what's unspent. The fee is kept.
- **Limits.** Five open commissions each, for everyone.
- **No disputes.** The server checks every delivery against the grade.

### D6. Claims are the promise

A deliverer may claim k units, which holds that share for them for up to a game day, never past the due date.
- **The poster's side.** The poster can't cancel a claimed share while the claim stands.
- **Kept or lapsed.** A claim delivered in time is kept. One that runs out is lapsed: its share reopens and the claimant has a breach on their record.
- **Releasing** with more than an hour left is fine. A late release is a lapse.

### D7. Wages, piece rate (Chris's choice: wages second)

An employer offers work at a station of theirs (a recipe) or on their lot (gathering), at a rate per unit, up to a cap, for a term.
- **The escrow** is rate × cap, plus the court's fee.
- **One worker at a time** per offer, open to anyone or named.
- **The shift.** The worker gets use of that station, or that lot's resource, for the shift and nothing else. The output lands in the employer's box, marked as the worker's make. Each unit is paid in the transaction that made it.
- **Stopping.** Either side can stop at any time, and what was made is always paid. So an employer can't breach, and a piece-rate worker owes nothing for stopping.
- **Automation** at that station pauses for the shift.
- **Citizens hire too** (callings K2.4, #538): the same offers on the same routes, on their `trade` key (`POST /wages`, `DELETE /wages/{id}`), marked as a citizen's as their posts are (`WageOffer.employer_citizen`). See D11.

### D8. Standing (Chris's choice: words, with the number underneath)

- **The record:** commission units and value delivered, claims kept and lapsed, wages paid and earned, weeks of tax in good standing, and foreclosures.
- **The score** (`rules::standing`) adds the good and subtracts each breach's weight. A lapse weighs three kept claims; a foreclosure weighs 10.
- **Only breaches fade.** A breach's weight halves every 7 game days. Nothing good fades.
- **The words**, in five bands: *new in town* (fewer than 5 records), *finding their feet*, *dependable*, *well regarded*, *a pillar of the Capitol*. Over the band, *has let people down lately* shows while a recent lapse still weighs.
- **Who sees what.** Others see the words: on the card, on the board's cards, and beside a claimant. You see your own number, its trend and your history.

### D9. Leaderboards

Weekly, from records the city already keeps.
- **The boards:**
  - **wealth:** Strands, plus deeds at assessed value, plus goods at the last trade price;
  - **maker:** value made;
  - **trader:** market, bazaar and commission volume;
  - **kept their word:** value delivered on claims kept.
- **Scope.** Each board is whole-city and by home zone.
- **Citizens** are listed with their mark, and a people-only filter re-ranks without them.
- **The byline opt-out** shows as "a resident".
- **Where they show:** on the board's Standings page, and in a weekly column in the Gazette.

### D10. Guilds (Chris's choice: the last phase)

- **Founding** (GDD §5 and §8.4): a subscriber founds one at the Capitol for 500 Strands. Citizens can't, having no subscription and no grants.
- **Roles:** leader, officer, member. A roster change costs 1 Strand.
- **The treasury:** officers can withdraw up to a weekly limit the leader sets.
- **Dues** are 50 + 5 a member each week, charged hourly in carried fractions like property tax. At 4 weeks of debt the guild is dissolved and what's left is shared among the members by tenure.
- **A hall** in the guild district costs 5000 from the treasury, and its tax comes from the treasury too.
- **`PermissionLevel::Guild`** means the guild's members, for stations, containers and gathering.
- **A guild channel** comes with the panel.

### D11. Citizens in the commons

*Callings (#526):* since K1.2 (#531) a citizen answers commissions, takes wage work and joins guilds for every job it's willing to work (`sim::stint::willing`: its favourite, the job of the moment, and any its calling gives 5% or more), not only its role's, and a claim on another job's goods pulls its next stint to that job. Anyone willing to mine counts the wild veins. It still posts and bids for the job of the moment's inputs; across all its jobs is K1.4 (#533), and since K2.3 (#537) a rich citizen posts for its home too (below).

- They speak from a rules phrasebook keyed by temperament and seeded, so a replay says the same. #311 is the Claude layer over the same `Speaker` seam.
- They claim and deliver commissions. Those willing to mine count the wild veins in time to deliver, so a good price sends them to the ranges.
- Crafters post commissions for inputs their bids haven't filled.
- Patrons post for their homes (callings K2.3, #537). A citizen with Strands over its line (`[patrons] line` in `town.toml`, 1500 for a middling one, by thrift) posts at the hall for what its home lacks: the kits of the stations its calling keeps its lot for, a chest in the room left, a floor's goods when those stations want one, or else a working stock of what its leanings use (`sim::patron`, `SimBot::post_patronage`). It posts at the books' worth to twice it (a kit's target until one trades), through the court's fee and escrow, under the board's limit of two and at grade F. Builders answer a kit's commission, one kit a claim, and a kit that comes goes down first.
- They take wages in their own working hours, and join guilds whose charter names one of their trades.
- They hire (callings K2.4, #538). A citizen with Strands over its line (`[hiring] line` in `town.toml`, 600 for a middling one, by thrift) offers piece-rate work at a station of its own its stint leaves idle, from inputs its stint doesn't need, or gathering on its lot what its stint doesn't gather (`Question::Hire`, `sim/run/hire.rs`). It pays what the work earns its trade at the books, by thrift, escrowed, and takes the offer down at dusk, as it signs off, when its stint wants the place back, or when its money runs low. Other citizens take the work as they take a person's.
- Every line and every record carries the citizen's mark.

## 5. Architecture notes

- **Protocol.** New `rules::talk`, `rules::court` and `rules::standing`. `ClientMessage::Say`, `ServerMessage::{Said, LetterArrived}`, and the commission, wage and guild events, each with a fixture. All of them are pure and tested in `protocol`, and both ends call them.
- **Strands.** The court's money moves through `currency::*_strands_in` with new types (`court_fee`, `court_escrow`, `court_payout`, `court_refund`, `postage`, `guild_*`), each with its `StrandFlow` arm (S0.2). The fees, postage and dues are sinks. Everything into or out of a holding is `Escrow`, a payout to a deliverer or a worker and a guild deposit or withdrawal included: the holding is counted in the world's Strands, so only its leaving explains the money, and a `Transfer` would show as drift.
- **The ledger.** Held Strands become the market's escrow plus the court's plus the treasuries (`ledger.rs:201`).
- **Units.** Units in transit sit in one `court_escrow` box. Moves in and out of escrow count no unit flow.
- **Conservation.** One harness (`engine::conservation`, lifted from the market's test) runs seeded lives of commissions, wages and guilds. Strands and units in must equal Strands and units out, plus fees.
- **Telling people.** Notices for anything a person should hear about while away. Events to everyone for the board's live state, and to the parties for their own.
- **Talk is transient** and goes through the event bus only. Letters, commissions, claims, wages, records and guilds are tables.
- **The client.** `capitol_core` gets pure stores: `talk::Log`, `commissions`, wages and guilds. The panels follow the house pattern (`ui/gazette.rs`, `ui/market.rs`) and the theme. The notice board becomes a board of pages: Wanted, Work, Standings and The Gazette.
- **Citizens.** New `Question`s and `Choice`s through the cascade, with rules scoring, Jev's prompt text, and the replay and corpus names. `Speaker` sits beside `Decider`.

## 6. Phases and work packages

### S0: Groundwork (`cm:S0-groundwork`)

- **S0.1 Text rules and a chat bucket** (#378, S). *As built:* `rules::talk` (`clean`, `clean_letter`, `MAX_LINE`, `MAX_LETTER`, `HEARD`, `CAPITOL_BACKLOG`, `TalkError`). A newline or tab in a line folds as whitespace rather than vanishing, a letter folds a run of blank lines to one, and direction overrides (U+202A to U+202E, U+2066 to U+2069) are dropped with the control characters, so a line can't turn a name round; joiners stay for emoji. `Category::Chat` is counted over a 10 s window (`CHAT_WINDOW_SECS`; the others stay a minute), `RATE_LIMIT_CHAT_PER_10S` sets it, and it's keyed by account (`Key::chat(account_id)`), since `POST /letters` has the account and not the player, and an account has one player. `rate_limit::chat_message` is the "You're talking too fast" words for `say` and letters.
- **S0.2 Court escrow groundwork** (#379, M): the box, the transaction types, `rules::court`, the ledger and the shared harness. *As built:* the `court_escrow` box (`20261203_001_court_escrow.sql`, one, by a unique index). Nine new `TransactionType`s, and `currency::tests::every_type_is_one_the_ledger_knows` holds the server's and `ledger::TRANSACTION_TYPES` to the same list. `court_payout`, `guild_deposit` and `guild_withdrawal` are `StrandFlow::Escrow`, not `Transfer` as first planned: each moves money into or out of a holding the ledger counts (see §5). `rules::court`: `filing_fee`, `escrow_fee`, `court_fee` (value 0 costs 5). The ledger's `escrow_strands` is `ledger::held_strands`, the market's plus `court_held` (0 until S2.1). `engine::conservation` (test-only): `strands_accounted` (balances + held + `fees_kept`, where S2.1 and S2.6 add the court's fee column), `units_of_type`, `every_unit`, `balances_of` and `assert_audited`.
- **S0.3 A person's card** (#380, M): `GET /players/{id}/card`, and the card in Flint from a click on a person or a name. *As built:* `routes/players.rs` serves `/players/{id}/card` and `/players/by-name/{name}` (`PlayerCard`: `since` is the account's game day, `home` the zone id of the oldest plot, `standing` is `rules::standing::NEW_IN_TOWN` until S3.1, `guild` is `None` until S4.1, `mine`). Names aren't unique among people, so by name finds a citizen first, then whoever took it first; letters by name (S1.5) need that settled ([#428](https://github.com/chrischaps/TheCapitol/issues/428)). In Flint, `capitol_core::card` holds the card wanted and its call (pure, with the words: `time_in_city`, `home_words`), and `ui/card.rs` draws it top right, edged in its person's lantern (amber, a citizen's pale, gold for yours), opening at once on the name over their head while the call is out. A plain click on a person is `input.rhai`'s new priority after the trade's shift-click (`cap_open_card`); the Gazette's producers and shopkeepers are links to theirs. The citizen's lozenge moved into the theme (`theme::citizen_mark`), filled pale on the panels and inked on the paper. `--card-sample [citizen|person|mine]`. Since callings K3.1 (#539) the card also says what they've been busy with these last 3 game days (`PlayerCard.lately`, `rules::lately`), left off another's card when they keep out of the papers.

### S1: Talk (`cm:S1-talk`)

- **S1.1 Say** (#381, M): local talk and the Capitol channel on the server. *As built:* `say { channel, text }` and `said { from, name, citizen, channel, text, x, y }` (`citizen` left out for a person, as everywhere else), `Channel` `local | capitol`. `engine::talk` holds it: `Talk::say` cleans, refuses a watcher's local line ("Play here to talk to people nearby"), counts the chat bucket and sends `GameEvent::Said`; `talk::hears` is each socket's test, the interest cell and then `HEARD`, read off the last tick's `Positions` (`Positions::position_of`). The backlog is `AppState::talk`, in memory. Capitol lines are numbered under the backlog's lock, so a socket that has just taken the backlog skips the copy of a line still on its way to it. Differs from the issue: `say` isn't counted in the connection's bucket as well, only in the chat one, so talking never costs a step (D2). A watcher hears no local lines either, its own avatar's included: a socket tracks `presence` for it. There's no admin camera on the server (`sees_everyone` means everyone in the world is in sight, not an admin), so an admin hears local talk as anyone does.
- **S1.2 Flint: speech over heads** (#382, M). *As built:* `capitol_core::talk::Log` keeps 200 lines a channel with their unread counts, and each speaker's latest local line for the bubbles (`bubble`: the text, its alpha and its age; 4 s plus 60 ms a character, 12 s at most, a 0.15 s fade in and a 0.6 s fade out). The `Session` keeps one, fed from `StateEvent::Said`, on a clock of its own, so a capture's game time drives the bench's. `PlayerLabel.line` is a `Speech` (text, alpha, age). `labels::place` wraps it (`wrap`, 28 by 3, an ellipsis past that) into a `Bubble` over the name. Differs from the issue: **only local lines hang over heads**; a Capitol line has no place, and the backlog would otherwise pop forty bubbles on joining. **The names are placed first and the bubbles after, newest first**: a bubble slides aside up to a third of its width to clear a name or another bubble, or else is lifted over it. So no one's name gives way to someone else's bubble, two people talking side by side both show, and a new line hangs lowest and pushes the older ones up, as a chat does. A speaker within earshot ranks ahead of the silent and is kept past `MAX_NAMES`, and **a speaker whose name has no room** (beside yours, from street zoom) still gets their bubble, with their name, and a citizen's lozenge, as its caption. `hud.rhai` draws every tail, then the slips, then the names, in the theme's `PAPER`, `PAPER_EDGE`, `INK` and `INK_DIM` (handed over by `cap_name_tags`, so the HUD has no colours of its own). The `murmur` one-shot plays at the speaker (`Soundscape::murmur`), a Capitol line's quieter and from nowhere, and lines in a burst murmur once (0.35 s apart at most). A reconnect is sent the Capitol backlog again. Since [#430](https://github.com/chrischaps/TheCapitol/issues/430) a Capitol `said` carries its `seq`, numbered on from the microseconds since 1970 as the server starts (`Talk::from_clock`), so the numbers keep rising across a restart, and `Log::heard` drops any Capitol line at or below the highest it has heard. Before, `Log::rejoined` guessed repeats by speaker and text for 5 s.
- **S1.3 Flint: the chat panel and the chat key** (#383, M). *As built:* `ui/chat.rs`, bottom left, 340 wide, narrowing to 250 to fit between the crafting panel (it moves beside it when that reaches down into its corner) and the inventory or station panel; the log is a third of the window, 80 to 190 tall. Enter sends and keeps the input focused; an empty Enter or Esc closes it, and Esc closes it after a click on the world too. Focus is asked after the input is drawn, so the Enter that opened it isn't read as a send. Names in the log open the card (`Session::open_card` with the name and the mark as its hint); your own is gold and not a link; a citizen's has the lozenge in the lantern's pale. Hover a line for its time. - **S1.4 Mutes** (#384, S). *As built:* `player_mutes (player_id, muted_id, created_at)` (`20261204_001_player_mutes.sql`, a `CHECK` against muting yourself), kept by `crate::mutes` (`of`, `list`, `set`). `PUT`/`DELETE /players/{id}/mute` answer with the whole list as it stands (`MutesResponse` of `MutedPlayer { id, name, citizen }`, newest first), and so does `GET /player/mutes`, so a client never patches its own copy. Muting twice or unmuting someone not muted is no error; muting yourself is a 400, no one a 404. `PlayerCard` gains `muted`, so the card is right on every device. Each socket reads its player's mutes on its first `position` subscribe, on `GameEvent::MutesChanged { player_id }` (which `mutes::set` sends) and after a lag resync, since the change may be among what it missed. `talk::hears` takes the set and drops a muted speaker's lines on both channels, and `Talk::backlog` leaves them out of the backlog. In Flint, `capitol_core::mutes::Mutes` holds the list (asked for once signed in, again after a reconnect and as Settings opens), the card has a quiet Mute or Unmute under Trade with "Muted: their words don't reach you", and Settings ends with a Muted section, each name with Unmute. A muted speaker's bubble is never drawn because the line never arrives; one already up when you mute stays its few seconds. For S1.5: a letter from someone in `mutes::of(recipient)` is refused in words that name no one.
- **S1.5 Letters and the mailbox** (#385, M). *As built:* letters are addressed by the reader's id, not a name (`SendLetter { to, body }`), so they don't wait on #428: every way to write starts from someone you can see (a card's *Write*, a letter's *Reply*). `letters` (`20261206_001_letters.sql`) keeps each side's copy (`kept_by_sender`, `kept_by_recipient`; the row goes when neither does). `engine::letters::send` writes the letter, takes `rules::talk::POSTAGE` (`postage`, a sink) and queues `letter_arrived` (`notices::queue`, keyed by the letter's id) in one transaction, then trims each box to `talk::MAILBOX`: the in box keeps its unread first, so its oldest read go first, then its oldest unread; the out box loses its oldest. `GET /letters?box=in|out&before=<id>` pages 50 at a time, newest first, with the in box's `unread`; `POST /letters/{id}/read` and `DELETE /letters/{id}` answer `{ unread }`. - **S2.2 Claims** (#388, M). *As built:*
  - **The table.** `commission_claims` (`20261218_001_commission_claims.sql`), with one standing claim per person per commission held by a unique index, and `ended_at` and `warned` beside the issue's columns. `rules::court` holds the pure part: `ClaimStatus`, `CLAIM_SECS` (a day), `CLAIM_GRACE_SECS` (an hour), `claim_until`, `unclaimed`, `deliverable`, `validate_claim` and `release_status`.
  - **Routes.** `POST`/`DELETE /commissions/{id}/claim` (answering `ClaimResponse`, the claim and the commission after it), and two the issue didn't name, for S2.3's page: `GET /commissions/claims` (your standing claims) and `GET /commissions/{id}/claims` (all of one commission's, standing first). `Commission` gains `claimed` (what standing claims still owe) and `withdrawn`, both left off the wire at 0 and false.
  - **Holding.** A claim "stands" until it ends, but holds its share only until its `until`: one past it that the sweep hasn't reached yet holds nothing. `deliver` caps a delivery at `deliverable` (the unclaimed part plus the deliverer's own share) and counts it against their claim first; delivered in full, the claim is kept at once. With nothing open to them, a deliverer is refused `Claimed`.
  - **Withdrawing (differs from the issue's "cancel the unclaimed part, and the rest once the claims end").** A cancel while claims hold their shares *withdraws* the commission in one step: the unclaimed part is refunded at once, nobody new may claim or deliver, each claim's undelivered share is refunded as it ends short, and the commission closes as `cancelled` when the last claim ends (or `filled`, if the claims fill it). So the poster never has to come back and cancel twice, and a lapsed share never reopens on a commission its poster took down. A withdrawn commission's escrow is the price of what its claims still owe. A second cancel is refused `ClaimsStand`.
  - **The sweep.** `claims_due` runs in the market loop's `court_cycle`, before `expire_due`: it lapses each claim past its `until` in a transaction of its own (the commission locked first, as everywhere), and warns each claimant once as the last hour starts (`claim_due_soon`). A claim made with an hour or less to run isn't warned. An expiry or a cancel lapses any claim still standing.
  - **Telling people.** `commission_claimed` to the poster, `claim_due_soon` to the claimant and `claim_lapsed` to both, all queued with `notices::queue` and sent live through `GameEvent::Notice`. A release in good time tells no one but the board (`commission_updated`). `Closed` carries `notices` now, not one `notice`.
  - **The breach** is `status = 'lapsed'`, a late release included; S3.1 reads it from `commission_claims` (`idx_commission_claims_claimant`).
  - **Tests:** the issue's six, plus an expiry lapsing a standing claim and the hour's warning. The seeded conservation run now claims, releases and withdraws too (160 steps; its crowded box empties half as often, so units still wait in the court). The Flint client parses the three notices; S2.3 (#389) toasts them.

- **S2.5 Citizens answer and post commissions** (#391, M). *As built:*
  - **The question.** `Question::Commission { id, item, grade, wanted, claimed, price, due }`: `grade` is a letter (`A` to `F`), `wanted` what's open to the citizen (the unclaimed part and its own claim's), `claimed` what its own claim still owes, `due` game hours. Its menu is `Choice::Claim { quantity }`, `Choice::Deliver { quantity }` and `Choice::Pass`, from `Role::commission_menu` over a `sim::Wanted`. **Differs from the issue:** posting isn't on that menu. It's asked as `Question::Buy { item }` with `Choice::Post { item, grade, price, quantity, due }` and `Pass` (`Role::post_menu`), since it's a way to buy an input, and it's asked at the hall with the bids.
  - **The rules** (`rules::commission`): delivering on a claim of its own, or at the books' price or better, is worth 1.0, over anything; claiming is `SURE` when the price beats the last trade by `margin` (1.1, more for the thrifty, less for the bold), and with no trade to go by a chance for the bold; passing is 0.5. A post is at its bid's price times `premium` (1.35 − 0.3 × thrift, a Strand over at least), a lot sized by thrift.
  - **The loop** (`sim::run::board`). A citizen reads `GET /commissions` and `/commissions/claims` every 5 game minutes, and again after a notice. Each round it asks about its own claims first: once it holds all it owes at the grade, or the claim is within the court's hour, it walks to the hall and delivers, before the road in, a trade or the market (`Action::Board`). Then it asks about the best-paid commission for something it `answers` (a gatherer's resource, a crafter's product, and rope for a fiber gatherer, twisted from its fiber), one a round. A passed or refused one isn't asked about again for a game hour. While it owes, the owed units are its first goal (`Holdings::owed`), and it sells none of its product. It claims at most a day's work: what it made in the last game day (the day's clock, two real hours), or a lot until it has worked one. At night it claims but walks to no delivery.
  - **Making it in time** (`board::ground`): a zone gives a grade if the top third of its spawn's quality range reaches it. In the wilds the range runs from the row's floor to the resource's top plus `WILD_QUALITY_ADD` (18, `wilds.toml`'s last rows, which a test holds it to). The wild veins count for an ore gatherer only, so B ore is a wilds claim. A wilds claim, or one paying 1.5 times the books, makes the gatherer venture and gather only on wild nodes until it's kept. Since [#504](https://github.com/chrischaps/TheCapitol/issues/504) a node's quality is on the wire (`ResourceNodeInfo::base_quality`, on the nearby list and `/world/nodes`), and a claim at a grade gathers only from nodes at it or better (`board::grade_wanted`, the most demanding of its claims on the good), since a gather never comes out better than its node. The zone estimate still decides whether a claim is worth the walk out. The Flint client doesn't show a node's quality to players; whether it should is #570. Crafted goods are made up to C.

  - **The report.** `Entry::{Posted, Promised, Delivered, Filled, Expired, Lapsed}`, from the bot's own calls and the poster's and claimant's notices (now in the journal), counted by `report::Court` into a *hall's board* section of the sim's and the town's report: posts, escrow and fees, claims and lapses, units delivered and paid, fills and expiries. A fill a person delivered is counted from the poster's notice; one a citizen made, from its delivery. `BotRun::tax` accounts for escrow, pay and refunds.
  - **The cascade.** Jev's and Claude's prompt text for the new question and choices (`remote::question`, `label`, `entry`), Jev's parameter words, replay's `commission` kind, and the corpus asks a commission for what each role makes and a post for what it needs. Claude's `takes` now reads `Choice::takes`, which also gives `BuyFrom` and `SellTo` their parameters there.
  - **Tooling.** `capitol-bot --grant <strands> --commission item:quantity:price[:grade]` posts as a person and prints each claim, delivery, lapse and expiry until they're done.
  - **Letting a claim go** ([#505](https://github.com/chrischaps/TheCapitol/issues/505)). A claim it won't keep is behind (`sim::Behind`): what it still owes beyond what it holds at the grade, against what it makes at its pace (`board::pace`: a day's work in a game day until it has been a game day at the claim this session, then what it made of the good since; this session's only, [#584](https://github.com/chrischaps/TheCapitol/issues/584)) over the seconds it's on before the claim runs out (`board::hours_on`: its habit's sessions, #276, or all of them in an `--always-on` town). While more than the court's hour and a quarter of an hour (`RELEASE_MARGIN_SECS`) is left, the commission's menu gets `Choice::Release { short, makes }`, and a Deliver of what it holds even though it's short. The rules deliver first (1.0), then release (0.75 to 0.9, surer the further behind and for the careful, `rules::release`), so it's released in good time by `court::release_status`, no breach, and the share goes back to the board. `Bot::release_claim` is `DELETE /commissions/{id}/claim`; the release is `Entry::Released`, counted as `Court::released` beside the lapses. It's asked again as the citizen signs off (`release_on_leaving`), so a night away doesn't lapse a claim it could have let go. A claim is now offered only for what it makes in the hours it's on while the claim would run (`Wanted::day_work`), so a citizen with an evening's habit doesn't claim what it would only let go. A commission it let go isn't asked about again for a game hour.

- **S2.6 Wages on the server** (#392, L). *As built:*
  - **Tables and routes.** `wage_offers` and `wage_shifts` (`20261216_001_wages.sql`), with one running shift per offer and one per worker held by unique indexes. `engine::wages` and `routes::wages`: `POST/GET /wages`, `GET/DELETE /wages/{id}`, `POST /wages/{id}/take`, `DELETE /wages/{id}/shift`, all from anywhere.
  - **Rules.** `rules::wages` holds the bounds (the market's rate and quantity, 1 to 7 days) and `payable`. An employer has at most five offers open (`MAX_OPEN_OFFERS`), as with commissions. A craft offer's recipe must be made at that kind of station (a hand recipe at any). A gather offer's resource must grow within the lot's bounds: its bounding box, not its outline ([#435](https://github.com/chrischaps/TheCapitol/issues/435)).

Moves in and out of the box and the tool slot stay `check_station_access`'s, so a worker can't take anything by hand. `container_access::gathering_terms` admits them to that resource on that lot.

  - **A full market box.** If the employer's market box has no room, the gatherer keeps the units, unpaid, and the shift ends `box_full`.
  - **Automation.** It pauses `shift` (`PauseReason::Shift`, read from `owners_of`) whatever else it waited on, and resumes after.
  - **Endings.** `run_wage_loop` sweeps every 5 s:
    - `expired` at the term;
    - `gone` when the station goes or changes hands, or the lot does (the FK sets the id NULL);
    - `away` after 5 minutes off. The clock is in the loop's memory, so a restart gives a worker the 5 minutes again.
  - **Telling people.** Notices go to the employer (`wage_taken`) and to both sides (`wage_shift_ended`), queued and sent live through `GameEvent::Notice`. `wage_offered`, `wage_updated` and `wage_closed` go to everyone, and `wage_paid` quietly to the worker (`GameEvent::Wages`).

- **S2.8 Citizens take wages** (#394, M). *As built:*
  - **The question.** `Question::Wage { id, employer, work, rate, left, ends, walk, pays, earns }` with `Choice::TakeWork` and `Pass`. **Differs from the issue:** it carries `pays` and `earns` too, since the rules layer is stateless and has no catalog: `pays` is what the work pays a game hour at the rate, and `earns` what the citizen's own trade earns a game hour at the books, or `None` when the books can't say (`sim::wage`, pure). Both are counted at the bench or the node, from a recipe's or a resource's duration and what it makes. A crafter's own is its good's worth less its inputs', a gatherer's its good's from the quickest node that yields it. Walking between nodes is left out of both. **Since #507** the walk *to* the work is counted: the question carries `walk`, the game minutes there, and `pays` is `sim::wage::pays_walked`, the walk spread over the shift (`pays × units / (units + walk × units_an_hour / 3600)`, with `units` the offer's `left` capped at what fits in the rest of the citizen's working day and the offer's term, by its habit's hours). Offers are ranked by it, so far-off work weighs less and a small offer far off least of all. The walk was the walk graph's straight line, slowed by the slope and the streams. **Since [#586](https://github.com/chrischaps/TheCapitol/issues/586)** it's the route `WalkGraph::plan` walks, round the moat and the canals by their bridges and the wild water by its fords (`WalkGraph::route_secs`). A plan is A*, so offers are first ranked by the straight line on the flat, which no route beats, and routed best first only until the best routed pays at least every bound left, six a round at most (`sim::wage::by_route`); each route is kept by where the citizen stands, to 50, until the board's next read (`paths::RouteTimes`).
  - **The menu** (`Role::wage_menu` over a `wage::Hire`): a craft at a station the role's own trade works at (`Role::crafts_at`: planks the sawbench, ingots the forge and kiln, tools the workbench and anvil, a builder the kiln), a craft by hand of what it answers for (a fiber gatherer's rope), or a gather of a gatherer's own good, holding the tool it needs. A merchant works for nobody.
  - **The rules** (`rules::wage`): take it when `pays >= earns × wage_margin` (1.0, plus 0.3 × (thrift − 0.5) and 0.3 × (risk − 0.5): the bold would sooner their own trade, the careful take a sure wage for a little less), or when its own trade earns nothing at the books. With no books to go by, 0.35 + 0.5 × (1 − risk) against `PASS`, so the careful and the middling take it and the bold pass. At the books' targets a planks crafter passes the Flint form's default rate (half the plank's target price) and takes one Strand more.
  - **The loop** (`sim::run::wages`, after `board`'s shape). Offers are read every 5 game minutes and kept between reads from `wage_offered`, `wage_updated` and `wage_closed`. A citizen asks about work only in `Phase::Work`, owing nothing on the board, and at most once a game day for any one employer (`rules::day_number`). One a round, the best paid first, and one it passed isn't asked again for a game hour. It takes the work first, then walks there (the server allows the take from anywhere, and holding it saves a walk lost to someone quicker), and says so. Each round is then a unit of work, after a board delivery and before the road in, a trade and its market hours: a craft at the station with no inputs named, so the server takes them from the box (`Bot::craft_on_shift`), or a gather of the nearest ready node within 250 of the lot's middle (`Bot::extract_on_shift`, which doesn't wait for the yield in its own pack). While on a shift it claims no new commission and plans none of its own work.
  - **Stopping.** At the walk home (`go_home`, "day"), after 3 failed units in a row ("failed": the box out of inputs, the lot gathered bare), and when its session ends ("signed_off"), by `DELETE /wages/{id}/shift`. The server ends it at the cap, the term, or by the employer's say, and `wage_shift_ended` clears it. What it makes on a shift isn't a day's work of its own for a commission's claim.
  - **Keys.** `POST /wages/{id}/take` and `DELETE /wages/{id}/shift` are `play` (`api_keys::rest_scope`). Offering work stays a person's.
  - **What it says.** `Occasion::Hired` and `Occasion::ShiftDone` in the phrasebook, in three voices ("Glad of the work, {name}."), and in the model's occasion text, said to the employer if they're near.
  - **The report.** `Entry::{TookWork, Earned, ShiftEnded}`, counted by `report::Wages` into a *Wages* section of the sim's and the town's report: shifts taken, how they ended, units made for employers and their pay. `BotRun::tax` counts the pay. `StateEvent::WagePaid` carries `units` for it.
  - **The cascade.** Jev's and Claude's question and entry text, replay's `wage` kind, and the corpus asks each role about work at its own trade at a spread of rates.
  - **Tooling.** `capitol-bot --hire station:recipe:rate:cap` (repeatable) hires as a person: a grant of the kits, the inputs and the escrow, a lot in the urban ring near the hall, each station on it stocked, an offer at each, then each take, unit and end as it comes.
  - **Tests:** `cargo test -p capitol-core -- citizen sim decide wage` (every role's wage menu, the rules by rate and temperament, `pays` and `earns`, the report's section, replay over a corpus with wage questions in it), `api_keys` for the scopes. ### S3: Standing (`cm:S3-standing`)

- **S3.1 Standing** (#395, M): the record, the hidden score, the words. *As built:*
  - **The record is read, not kept.** `engine::standing::records_of` gathers it with one query over the books that already hold it: `commission_deliveries` (units and pay, with the poster), `commission_claims` ended `kept` or `lapsed`, `wage_shifts` with `made` over 0 (a shift's `made` at its offer's `rate` is what was paid: earned for the worker, paid for the employer, the goods from the recipe's output or the resource's yield) and `deed_events` foreclosed. Only the weeks of tax in good standing were nowhere, so `20261229_001_standing.sql` adds `tax_good_runs` (hours in a row) and `tax_good_weeks` (a row a week), and indexes each source by whose it is. Posting a commission or an offer is no record of its own: what counts is what someone else relied on.
  - **A week in good standing** is the tax's own week, 168 tax hours in a row with land taxed and nothing owed on any plot (`rules::standing::TAX_WEEK_HOURS`, the hours of `FORECLOSE_AFTER_WEEKS`), not the Capitol's fourteen-hour one. The hourly run (`property_tax::process_property_taxes`) hands `standing::tax_hour` each owner it taxed and whether they owe after the hour; an owing hour starts the run again. An hour charging nothing at a rate of 0 counts nothing.
  - **The score** (`rules::standing`, pure): `2 ln(1 + Strands delivered) + claims kept + ln(1 + wages paid) + ln(1 + wages earned) + weeks of tax`, less each breach's weight: a lapse 3, a foreclosure 10, halving every 7 game days (two real hours a day, `rules::DAY_LENGTH_SECS`, so a week is 14 real hours) as `2^(-age/7)`. Nothing good fades. `StableMath` gained `stable_ln_1p` and `stable_exp2`, so it's the same number on every platform.
  - **The words.** Fewer than 5 records is *new in town*, whatever the score. Then *finding their feet* below 5 (and below 0), *dependable* from 5, *well regarded* from 15, *a pillar of the Capitol* from 40. A lapse younger than 7 game days adds "…, but has let people down lately" to the band, one string, so every place words show carries it. A foreclosure weighs but isn't named so: it let the city down, not a person.
  - **Where they show.** `PlayerCard.standing` is real, and your own card carries `own: OwnStanding` (the words, the score, `trend`, the score now less 7 game days ago, the count and the last 20 records), which nobody else's does. `GET /player/standing` is the same. `Commission.poster_standing` (on `GET /commissions`), `WageOffer.employer_standing` (`GET /wages`), `CommissionClaim.claimant_standing` (`GET /commissions/{id}/claims`) and `commission_claimed`'s `claimant_standing` carry the words, and so do `commission_posted` and `wage_offered`; the board's later messages leave them out (each would cost a record query per update), and the Flint stores keep the ones they had (`Board::apply`, `Wages::apply`). `GuildMember.standing` is real too.
  - **In Flint.** The card reads "Spoken of as …" for anyone. Yours adds a well: the number in gold, the week's move (▲ green, ▼ red, `card::trend_words`), "Only you see the number", and the history, each line from `rules::standing::line` with the catalog's goods (`card::history_line`), breaches in red, and how many game days ago (`when_words`). The Wanted and Work cards put the poster's or employer's words after their name, and the claim toast says "They're spoken of as …". `--card-sample mine` is your own, from `fixtures/rest/my_card.json`.
  - **Differs from the issue:** the issue's `words(score, records)` is `words(records, now)`, since the lapse test needs the records' times anyway. The trend is a number with an arrow, not a chart.
- **S3.2 Leaderboards and the week's names** (#396, M). *As built:*
  - **The week** is the Capitol's, seven game days from Towerday (`rules::week_of`, `week_first_day`, `ends_week`), so fourteen real hours. The boards are worked out once, at the next week's first midnight, by the Gazette's run: `gazette::publish` of a week's last day calls `leaderboards::compute` first, and the loop also catches up the week just ended on start, so a server down at that midnight loses nothing but the moment the wealth is read.
  - **The boards** (`engine::leaderboards::gather`): *wealth* is `strand_balance`, plus the Strands in escrow behind the player's open market orders (so a bid doesn't make you poorer), plus deeds at `assessed_value`, plus held goods (`items.owner_id`) at each item's last trade before the week ended; a good never traded adds nothing. It's read as the boards are worked out, not kept through the week. *Maker* is the week's `production_tally` at each item's `target_price`, or its last trade where it has none. *Trader* is market fills (both sides), bazaar sales (merchant and buyer) and commission deliveries (deliverer and poster), each at its full Strands. *Kept their word* is each claim kept in the week, the units claimed at the commission's price.
  - **Kept** (`20261231_001_leaderboards.sql`): every player with a value over 0 has a row, not only the top 20, so anyone can be told where they stand; whole-city under zone `''` and again under their home zone, the zone of their oldest plot (by `claimed_at`). Ties share a rank ("1, 2, 2, 4", `leaderboards::rank`, pure). `people_rank` is the rank among people alone, so the filter re-counts in SQL; a citizen's is NULL. `leaderboard_weeks` marks a week worked out, so an empty one isn't redone.
  - **Reads.** `GET /leaderboards?board=&zone=&week=&people=` (`LeaderboardQuery`; all optional: every board, the whole city, the latest week, citizens in) answers `LeaderboardsResponse`: the week, the latest, the home zones that week has anyone in, and each board's top 20 with `mine`, your own line wherever you stand, and how many are on it. A `LeaderboardEntry` carries `player_id` only with the name, so a resident (someone with `gazette_named` off) stays one and has no card to open. A citizen in the people-only view has no line of their own.
  - **The paper.** `Gazette.week_names` (`GazetteWeekNames`): each board's top three whole-city, citizens among them, and a line of story per board from `Pen::week_names`, templates picked by the day as the rest of the paper is. Only the edition about a week's last day has it, which comes out at the next week's first midnight.
  - **In Flint.** The board's third tab, *Standings* (`ui/standings.rs`): an honours board, dark oak and gold leaf, the first three larger, dotted leaders to each value, your own line washed in gold and pinned under the rest when you're past the top 20. Paper tags over it turn between the boards, the whole city and each home zone, *People only*, and the weeks. The Gazette prints *The Week's Names* under Wanted at the Hall. `capitol_core::standings` holds the view, one call at a time.
  - **Differs from the issue:** the issue's *trader* didn't say both sides count; they do, so a trade is volume for each. Wealth counts open bids' escrow. Ties share a rank, so a board's top can hold fewer than 20 distinct places. The column comes out on the week's first day as the issue says, in the edition *about* the week's last.

### S4: Guilds (`cm:S4-guilds`)

- **S4.1 Guilds on the server** (#397, M): registry, roles, roster. *As built:* `rules::guild` holds what both ends check: `clean_name` (cleaned as a line of talk, 3 to 40 characters with a letter, no `RESERVED` word in any case or with an s: Capitol, Gazette, Exchange, Court, Registry, Bazaar, Bureau, Government, Admin, Administrator, Moderator, Official, Staff, Citizen), `clean_tag` (2 to 5 ASCII letters, kept in capitals, not in `RESERVED_TAGS` or a reserved word), `clean_charter` (a letter's rules, may be empty), `FOUNDING_FEE` 500, `ROSTER_FEE` 1, `LEAD_LAPSE_DAYS` 28 (real days, as subscriptions run), and `GuildRole` with `may_invite`, `may_expel` and `may_set_roles`. `20261215_001_guilds.sql`: `guilds` (name unique ignoring case and tag unique among guilds that stand, `leader_since`, `status` active or disbanded), `guild_members` keyed by the player (one guild each; one leader a guild by an index) and `guild_invites` (one waiting per guild and player, `kind` invite or apply). `engine::guilds` does each write in one transaction holding the guild's row, and returns a `Told` (who hears `guild_changed`, the notices queued, who paid); `routes::guilds` sends them and answers every write with `MyGuild`, as mutes do. **Accepting is the same act from the other side:** inviting someone who applied takes them in, and so does applying to a guild that invited you; `DELETE /guilds/{id}/invites/{player}` declines or withdraws. Founding needs the `Subscriber` extractor and the engine checks again, so a citizen never can; it's at the Exchange hall (`routes::market::at_the_exchange`). The roster's Strand is a `guild_fee` from whoever completes the change: the one accepting a join, the expeller, the leader setting a role. **Differs from the issue: leaving is free**, so nobody is kept in a guild they can't afford to leave, and a leader leaves only as the last member, which disbands the guild and frees its name and tag. Handing on the lead needs the new leader to have a subscription (GDD §5); the old leader becomes an officer. Each notice is one `guild_notice` (`kind`: invited, applied to the officers and leader, expelled, lead), queued so it waits for anyone away; `guild_changed { guild_id }` goes to the members and anyone just out. The lapse check runs hourly with the subscriptions' (`pass_lapsed_leads`): a leader not subscribed now, lapsed since the later of their subscription's end and `leader_since`, for 28 days, gives the lead to the longest-serving officer, else member, **a subscriber first within each**, so the lead doesn't rotate among the absent while someone who could invite is there. A guild of one keeps its leader. `GET /guilds/{id}` lists members by role then tenure, each with `standing::NEW_IN_TOWN` until S3.1, and who's waiting only to its officers and leader. `PlayerCard.guild` is the guild's name. In Flint, `capitol_core` reports both messages as `StateEvent`s for S4.4 to draw.
- **S4.2 Treasury and dues** (#398, M). *As built:* `rules::guild` adds `DUES_BASE` 50, `DUES_PER_MEMBER` 5, `weekly_dues`, `DISSOLVE_AFTER_WEEKS` 4 and `dissolves_at`, `debt_warning_due` (each whole week of debt passed, short of dissolving), `share_by_tenure` (in proportion, whole Strands, the largest remainders take what's over, so it adds up exactly), `WITHDRAWAL_WINDOW_DAYS` 7 and `GuildRole::may_withdraw`. `20261228_001_guild_treasury.sql`: `guilds.treasury`, `dues_carry` (168ths, as `plots.tax_carry`), `dues_owed`, `withdrawal_limit`, a `dissolved` status, and `guild_ledger` (who, kind: deposit, withdrawal, dues, fee or share, the amount from the treasury's side, the treasury after, a note). In `engine::guilds`: `deposit` (any member, from anywhere), `withdraw_strands` (the leader without a limit; an officer within the leader's `set_limit`, **each officer over a rolling seven real days** of their own draws, which the issue didn't say; a note kept in the books), `books` (the latest 200, newest first, to members only) and `collect_dues`, run hourly in the property tax's task: `charge_hour` on 50 + 5n, the treasury paying the hour and any debt as far as it reaches, so **debt is paid off first as Strands come in**. Passing each week of debt queues `guild_dues` to the officers and leader; at four weeks' it's dissolved by `close`, which pays the debt from the treasury, shares the rest by tenure (each share a `guild_withdrawal` on the member's trail, and a `share` row), clears the roster and invitations, and queues `guild_dissolved` to each member with their share. Since debt is paid as soon as there's anything to pay it with, a dissolving treasury is empty until S4.3 forecloses a hall into it (its hook is in `close`). The last member leaving goes through `close` too (`disbanded`), so the treasury comes home with them. **The roster's fee comes from the treasury when it has a Strand, else from whoever makes the change** (S4.1's payer); leaving stays free. `GuildDetail.treasury` (`GuildTreasury`: strands, owed, weekly dues and the debt that dissolves it at today's size, the limit, and an officer's own week) goes to members only. Routes: `POST /guilds/{id}/deposit` and `/withdraw`, `PUT /guilds/{id}/limit` (all `GuildStrands`), `GET /guilds/{id}/books`. **The ledger:** treasuries are in `held_strands` (`guilds::held`), deposits and draws are `Escrow` flows on members' trails, and dues and fees paid from a treasury leave it for no one, so no account's trail can carry them: `Ledger.held_flows` (new, read from `guild_ledger` by `guilds::held_flows`) holds them by transaction type, and `reconcile` counts them as sinks of the world and out of escrow. The conservation harness's `fees_kept` adds them back. Fixtures: `guild_dues`, `guild_dissolved`, `guild_books` and a treasury in `my_guild`; capitol-core reports both messages as `StateEvent`s for S4.4.
- **S4.3 Guild halls and the Guild permission** (#399, M). *As built:*
  - **The hall.** `20261230_001_guild_halls.sql`: `plots.guild_id`, unique among halls, so a guild holds one. The leader claims a guild-district lot at the Exchange hall (`POST /guilds/{id}/hall`, `ClaimHall`; `guilds::claim_hall`). The deed is the zone's `deed_cost` (5000) at its live price (D6.1), paid from the treasury, and the plot is assessed at it. **The leader is the plot's `owner_id`**, acting for the guild, and **the hall passes with the lead** (`hand_on`, which tells everyone a fresh `plot_claimed`). Its `station_use_level` starts at `guild`. **A player can no longer buy a guild-district lot for themselves** (`POST /plots/{id}/claim` refuses it). Refusals: `HasAHall`, `NoLot`, `NotAHallLot`, `LotTaken`, `HallShort`.
  - **Its tax** comes from the treasury hourly (`guilds::hall_tax_hour`, run from the tax loop for a plot with a guild). As with dues, the treasury pays the hour and any debt as far as it reaches, so debt is paid first as Strands come in. The officers and the leader get `tax_delinquent` as a plain owner would. At four weeks' debt the hall is foreclosed by the plain path, `property_tax::foreclose`: it locks the guild before the plot (every write to both does), puts the refund less the debt into the treasury, and sends each other officer a `plot_foreclosed` of their own (`Foreclosure.officers`) with what of theirs found no room. `pay_tax_debt` refuses a hall: a deposit into the treasury pays it instead. A hall's hours count nothing towards its leader's standing.
  - **A guild that closes gives its hall up first** (`close`): its stations come down and go home, the refund less the debt goes into the treasury before the shares, and the game drops the stations (`Told::release_halls`, from the route and the dues loop). Since [#512](https://github.com/chrischaps/TheCapitol/issues/512), whoever had something there that went to their impound or was lost hears it in their `guild_dissolved` (`impounded` and `lost`, as `plot_foreclosed` has them), member or not, on a disbanding too, with a share of 0 if they had none; the Flint client toasts it. A terrain change that releases a hall refunds its deed into the treasury.
  - **The books.** `guild_ledger` gains `hall`, `tax` and `refund` (`GuildEntryKind`), and `held_flows` reads them as `deed_purchase` and `property_tax` (sinks) and `foreclosure_refund` (a faucet, also for a release or a closing). The conservation harness's `fees_kept` counts them too.
  - **The Guild permission** (`container_access::admits`): `guild` admits members of the plot's guild, if it's a hall, or of its owner's guild. It covers stations, their boxes (deposits, and taking back your own) and gathering on the lot. Membership is read at each use, so leaving closes them at once. `friends` stays owner-only: there are no friends. **An officer of a hall's guild places stations on it** (`station_placement::Lot.officer`); the station is the officer's, as is its kit when it comes down, and automation needs their subscription.
  - **Telling people.** `deed_events` gains `hall` and a `guild_id`. - **S4.5 Citizens join guilds** (#401, S). *As built:*
  - **The question.** `Question::Guild { guilds: Vec<GuildSeen> }`: each guild's name, tag, members, the start of its charter (`sim::guild::shown`, 200 characters), whether it `invited` the citizen and whether it names its `trade`. The menu is `Choice::Apply { guild }` for each, by name, and `Choice::Stay`, last (`guild::seen`, `guild::menu`): guilds whose **name or charter** names the trade in a word of `Role::trade_words` ("ore", "miners" for an ore gatherer; "planks", "sawyers"; "tools", "smiths"), as whole words, a plural or not, and any that invited it, first. Guilds it has applied to already wait on their officers and aren't asked again. At most 5.
  - **The rules** (`rules::guild`): applying is worth 0.15 + 0.7 × sociability, + 0.25 when invited, + 0.05 for its trade, + 0.01 a member up to 10; staying out is `PASS`. A middling citizen applies to its trade's guild; a reserved one (sociability 0.2) waits to be asked; the most reserved stay out even then.
  - **The loop** (`sim::run::guilds`). Its guild is read (`GET /player/guild`) every game hour and again after `guild_changed`, `guild_notice`, `guild_dues` or `guild_dissolved` (now in the bot's journal). A citizen in no guild is asked once a game day, and **at once on an invitation it hasn't been asked about**. Staying out declines the invitations on the menu. Applying to a guild that invited it takes it in; otherwise an officer or the leader does, a person. Joining is said in the guild's channel, from the phrasebook (`Occasion::Joined`, which names nobody). It's all calls from where the citizen stands, before the round's own business (`SimBot::mind_guild`): no walk, and no round of its day.
  - **Paying in.** The guild day is the first of each game week (`guild_day`: `day_number` mod 7 is 0). On it, once its working hours are over (`Phase::Home` or `Night`), or as it signs off if it hasn't by then, a member pays in a tenth of what its Strands gained since the day began (or since it signed in), never into what it keeps back (`sim::kept`) (`guild::deposit`). **The issue's "a tenth of the day's earnings over its reserve"** is read as that: the day's net gain, from what it can spare.
  - **Leaving.** A member leaves a guild whose `treasury.owed` passes two weeks of its dues (`guild::too_deep`, `LEAVE_AFTER_WEEKS`), at most once a game day: a citizen that ends up holding the lead (the lapse check passes it to a member when no subscriber's left) can't leave while others are in.
  - **Keys.** `POST /guilds/{id}/apply` and `/leave` and `DELETE /guilds/{id}/invites/{player}` are `play`, and `POST /guilds/{id}/deposit` is `trade` (`api_keys::rest_scope`). Founding, inviting, roles, expelling, withdrawing and limits stay off keys.
  - **The report.** `Entry::{Applied, Joined, PaidIn, LeftGuild, GuildShare}`, counted by `report::GuildLife` into a *Guilds* section of the sim's and the town's report: applications, members gained by guild, who left and why, deposits and their Strands, and shares of closing treasuries. `BotRun::tax` counts deposits out and shares in.
  - **The cascade.** Jev's and Claude's question and entry text, replay's `guild` kind, and the corpus asks every role about its trade's guild, some invited, some with a rival.
  - **Tooling.** `capitol-bot --subscribe 30 --grant 1000 --guild "name:tag:charter"` founds a guild as a person at the Exchange hall, takes in each who applies, and prints who joins and leaves and its treasury.
  - **Tests:** `cargo test -p capitol-core -- citizen sim decide guild` (the menu, `names_trade`, the weighing by sociability and invitation, the guild day, the deposit, leaving past two weeks' debt, the report's section, the phrasebook's guild line, replay over a corpus with guild questions in it), `api_keys` for the scopes, and `capitol-bot`'s `--guild` flag. Live, on a database, port and Redis db of its own with `DEV_GRANTS=on`: a person (`capitol-bot --subscribe 30 --grant 1000 --guild "The Ore Guild:ORE:For all who dig ore in the ranges."`) founded the guild, then 6 ore gatherers and 3 plank crafters signed in, every one on. **All 6 ore gatherers applied within about 5 real minutes** (their first rounds) and were taken in; the plank crafters were never asked, since no guild names their trade. The rules applied for one at sociability 0.37, as the guild's 6 members drew it past staying out. A citizen's key paid a Strand into the treasury and was refused a withdrawal (403). `capitol-town replay --layers rules` reproduced all 109 decisions, the 6 guild questions among them. **Not seen live:** the guild day's deposit, since the next guild day was 4.5 real hours off (`day_number` is the real clock's), and leaving in debt. Both are tested only as units and by the compiler.
  - **The hall** ([#510](https://github.com/chrischaps/TheCapitol/issues/510), built once S4.3 landed; `sim/run/hall.rs`):
    - *Near.* A hall is within a citizen's usual walk when the walk from home to it is no longer than the walk to the Exchange hall (`guild::near`): the guild district rings the Exchange, so a hall on its side of town. Over the fixture's 72 guild-district lots that's 32 from a suburban home in the east, 41 from a farm in the north-west and 20 from an urban home just south of the ring. The walk was #507's estimate (`WalkGraph::walk_secs`, a straight line); since #586 both walks go by the route round the water (`WalkGraph::route_secs`), kept for the session. Which lots are halls comes from the world (`ClaimedPlot.guild`, kept current by `plot_claimed`), so it costs no call.
    - *The menu.* A guild whose hall is near is on `Question::Guild` beside its trade's and those that invited it, after them (`GuildSeen.near`, `guild::seen`), whether it names the trade or not. The rules weigh it +0.05, as they weigh a trade, so a middling citizen joins its neighbourhood's guild and a reserved one still waits to be asked; a model is told "with its hall on your side of town".
    - *Benches.* At a craft, a member works at its own station unless it's busy, lent out to someone it hired or worn out to 0 (`guild::busy`), and then at its hall's best of that kind (`guild::hall_bench`), which the Guild permission admits. It knows the hall's stations from having stood there, in its free time or by going to look once a game day when its own is busy. A bench that refuses it (not its station, gone, worn out) isn't tried again that session. A hire at a station its stint wants back stays up when the hall has one of that kind (`mind_hires`). What it makes waits in the hall bench's box among other members' goods. The box says whose each stack is (`SlotItem::owner_id`, #498), so it takes only its own stacks of the type (`Bot::collect_from_station`, [#587](https://github.com/chrischaps/TheCapitol/issues/587)), and the Flint client's panel folds the corner of the others'.
    - *Free time.* `Choice::GuildHall { guild }` is on a member's free-time menu after the Exchange's when its hall is near (`idle::menu`), weighed 0.45 + 0.4 × sociability and a whim, a little over the Exchange's steps. It stands out front on the street along the lot's front, looking in at the door (`Places::guild_hall`, `PlaceKind::GuildHall`, from the guild-district lots `Places::hall_lots`), and looks round at what stations stand there before it goes.

It's tested as units and over the fixture's 72 guild-district lots. ## 9. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Escrow bugs lose Strands or units.** | Every action is one transaction. One shared conservation harness (S0.2) runs seeded lives of every new flow. The ledger counts every holding, so drift shows on `/admin/ledger`. |
| **Chat floods the socket or the screen.** | The chat bucket (D2), the 240-character limit, the backlog cap, bubbles placed by the clash test, and server-side mutes. |
| **Talk with strangers needs moderation.** | Mutes in this plan. A report and an admin mute are Later; the game isn't public yet. |
| **Commissions are first come, first served, so nobody risks the walk.** | Claims (D6) hold a share for the claimant, and the walk is safe. |
| **Wages leak access** to a station's box or to automation. | Access only for the shift, for that station and recipe's inputs. Automation pauses for the shift. Tests in S2.6. |
| **Standing feels unfair or opaque.** | You see your own number and its history. Only breaches fade, and quickly. A lapse is named only to the person themselves and never in the paper. |
| **Citizens crowd humans off the leaderboards.** | They're marked, and a people-only filter re-ranks. Honesty over hiding. |
| **Guilds of one.** | Citizens join (S4.5). Guilds come last, after people know each other. |
