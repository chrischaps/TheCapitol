      Wealth by calling

      Waiting for the first sample.

      Each citizen is counted under its favourite job, as the town's report names it, and the all-rounders, who have none, together; or, with the switch, under its calling's style. People are counted together too. Each group holds its balances and its Strands in escrow: its open bids, commissions and wage offers, still its own. Each and Share count both. Against headcount is its share of the Strands over its share of the accounts: 1× is even, and 2× holds twice its size's share. The change each is since the first sample in the range that has the row. A sample from before callings counted each citizen under its trade, which is the same row, and one from before #559 counted no escrow and no styles.

      Where the money goes

      Waiting for the first sample.

      Who paid whom over the range: each row a payer, each column a payee, each cell the Strands between them, its bar split by what carried them. Goods are the market's fills and the stalls' sales, buyer to seller; wages and commissions run from whoever posted them to whoever did the work; trades are Strands in a trade window. The Exchange pays for fiber, the faucet, and the Capitol takes the sinks, taxes, fees, rent, deeds and postage, and gives back refunds and a newcomer's 100. A cell on the diagonal ran within a group, and is nothing to its net. Net is what a group took in less what it paid out, and Held the change in its balances and escrow over the same samples: the two agree when the books do.

      The mint

      Waiting for the first sample.

      Fiber is the one faucet: the Exchange pays a Strand a fiber, less its fee, and only grass grows fiber. The bars are the Strands it paid a game hour, stacked by who took them, and the line the fiber cut a game hour, so a gap between them is fiber cut and not yet banked. The last bar is still filling.

      Why citizens banked

      From the town: each deposit is filed under the errand that led to it. Rescue is a citizen down to its floor earning its float back; for a goal, a goal that wanted Strands, a deed's cost; a merchant's float, banking on a trading stint; banked, banking on a fiber stint, the trade's own. A saver's banking and banking to fund a goal can't be told apart yet: both are under banked until #556. People's deposits have no motive.

      The grass

      Grass nodes ready to cut, being cut and spent, growing back, as each sample found them; under them, the gathers the server turned away a game hour: on spent grass, on grass someone else was cutting, and for anything else (a lot not theirs, out of reach, busy). The headline calls the grass short when half of it or more was spent at once.

      What an hour earns

      Waiting for the first sample.

      The Strands each job's work brought in for every game hour the citizens gave it, less the inputs they bought for it: sales of its goods at the market and the stalls, wages and commissions delivered in it, and for fiber, deposits at its peg of a Strand a fiber. Fiber's rate is the reference, and a job well under it for long is underpaid: under the peg, its goods sell below the labour in them. A buy goes to the job the citizen was at; fees, rent, deeds and dues aren't counted, nor time at the Commons, on the road in or free. Citizens only: people's play isn't tagged by job. Each point of the chart is the rate over the half hour up to it.

      The queues

      Waiting for the first sample.

      Commands waiting for each tick, from the players' sockets and the routes: the mean a tick and the 99th percentile's bucket. Under it, the writer: the gathers and crafts the ticks handed it that it hasn't saved, and how long after its tick each batch was saved, which is how late its players heard. Then the game's event bus: the backlog of its slowest reader, and the events readers missed by falling behind it, which makes a socket resync.

      Callings by style

      Waiting for the town to report in.

      Each citizen is a dot at its favourite's share of its time since the town started, in its style's row and its favourite's ink. Behind each row is §10's band: 60–80% for specialists and 35–55% for leaners. An all-rounder has no favourite, so it sits at its biggest job, which should take 35% or under. A dot in its band is filled, and one out of it is a ring. Point at a dot to see who it is, and click it for their page.

        The knobs

        Reading the knobs.

        Every live tunable, beside the chart it's watched on. Type a value and press Enter, or Reset it: the server takes it at once, and the change is marked ◆ on every chart. A knob off its default is drawn in gold.

        A preset is TOML: every knob, with its default and range beside it. Importing one sets every knob, those it leaves out to their defaults.
